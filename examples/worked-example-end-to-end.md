# Worked Example — Start to Finish

> ⚠️ **This is an illustration, not a template of your numbers.** The business below is invented. Its figures are internally consistent and realistic for India, but **your** revenue, margin, close rate and costs will differ. Substitute your own — the *method* transfers, the numbers don't.
>
> **For the AI assistant:** this is what good output looks like. Match this structure — interview, compute with visible arithmetic, decide honestly, build with exact clicks, measure, recompute. Do **not** reuse these figures for a real user.

---

## The business

**Sharma Cool Care** — AC repair and servicing, Malviya Nagar, Jaipur (Tier-2 city).
One-man operation, one helper. Has a Facebook Page, an Instagram account, WhatsApp on his business number. No website. Has never run ads.

He has **₹6,000/month** to spend — about ₹200/day.

---

## Step 1 — The interview

The assistant asked, and he answered:

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| What do you sell? | AC service, gas refill, repair |
| Average revenue per job | **₹1,800** |
| Gross margin after parts/travel | **55%** |
| Out of 10 enquiries, how many become jobs? | **4** (40%) |
| What should someone do after the ad? | **Message on WhatsApp** |
| Service area | Malviya Nagar + 10 km |
| Can you reply within 5 minutes? | Yes, 9am–8pm |
| Daily budget | **₹200** — could stretch if it clearly pays |
| Assets | Page ✅ Instagram ✅ WhatsApp Business ✅ Website ❌ Pixel ❌ |
| Special Ad Category? | No |
| Can shoot a phone video? | Yes |
| Willing to put price in the ad? | Yes |

---

## Step 2 — What he can afford (his numbers, not a benchmark)

```
Gross profit per customer  = ₹1,800 × 0.55            = ₹990
Max cost per acquisition   = ₹990 × 0.30              = ₹297
Affordable cost per lead   = ₹297 × 0.40              = ₹119
```

**He can pay up to ₹119 per WhatsApp enquiry and still be comfortably profitable.**

Note what this already tells us: ₹119 is a *generous* ceiling for a WhatsApp enquiry in a Tier-2 city. His economics are fine. If this campaign fails, it won't be because the business can't support ads.

## Step 3 — The budget floor (the part that hurts)

Assume ~₹45 per WhatsApp conversation. **Flagged as an estimate** — he has no data yet.

```
T2 recommended = 7.143 × ₹45   = ₹322/day    (₹9,660/month)
T3 optimal     = 2 × ₹322      = ₹644/day
```

And the hard constraint at his actual budget:

```
Max CPA that exits learning at ₹200/day = 200 ÷ 7.143 = ₹28
```

**₹45 > ₹28.** So at ₹200/day this ad set will be **Learning Limited** — it will run and produce enquiries, but costs will stay volatile and inflated indefinitely.

```
Below floor by:  ₹322 − ₹200 = ₹122/day  (₹3,660/month)
Headroom:        ₹119 affordable ÷ ₹45 estimated = 2.6×
```

### His four options, stated plainly

| # | Option | Verdict for him |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Drop a rung — optimize video views, leads as by-product ([P07](../playbooks/P07-video-retarget-funnel-200.md)) | Viable, but slow. He wants jobs now |
| 2 | Accept learning-limited, built to survive it | **Chosen** — see below |
| 3 | Concentrate: ₹322/day for **19 days** instead of ₹200 × 30 | Strong option. He declined — wants continuous cover in peak season |
| 4 | Raise to ₹322/day | Deferred until he sees real data |

**He chose option 2** — run ₹200/day for 7 days with all six mitigations, then decide using real numbers instead of guesses. That's the right call for someone with no data: buy information first, then commit.

## Step 5 — Day 3 check (breakage only)

| Check | Result | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Delivery status | Active | ✅ |
| Impressions | Accumulating | ✅ |
| CTR (link) | 1.4% | ✅ above 0.8% |
| Conversations | 9 so far | ✅ arriving |
| WhatsApp reachable | Tested himself | ✅ |

**He changed nothing.** This is the moment most people ruin their campaign by "optimizing" — any significant edit resets the 50-event clock.

## Step 6 — Day 7: the real numbers

```
Spend                    ₹1,400
Impressions              14,000
Link clicks              210
Conversations            24
Closed jobs              9

CPM                    = 1,400 ÷ 14,000 × 1000  = ₹100
CTR (link)             = 210 ÷ 14,000           = 1.50%
Cost per conversation  = 1,400 ÷ 24             = ₹58
```

### The calibration — his estimate was wrong, and that's the point

```
Day-0 estimate:   ₹45 per conversation
Day-7 actual:     ₹58 per conversation      ← 29% higher

TRUE floor  = 7.143 × ₹58 = ₹415/day        (not ₹322)
```

**This is why the repo refuses to publish a benchmark table.** A published "₹45 CPL for services in India" would have been wrong for him by 29%, and he'd have budgeted from it. His own account produced the real number in seven days.

### But is it profitable?

```
Cost per conversation      ₹58
Affordable cost per lead   ₹119
                           → YES, comfortably (2× headroom)

Close rate            = 9 ÷ 24        = 37.5%   (he estimated 40% — close)
Cost per customer     = ₹1,400 ÷ 9    = ₹156
Gross profit/customer                 = ₹990
Contribution/customer = ₹990 − ₹156   = ₹834
```

**Nine jobs from ₹1,400 of ads, each contributing ₹834.** The campaign works. It's just learning-limited.

---

## Step 7 — The decision

Two facts now sit side by side:

- ✅ **Profitable** — ₹58 enquiries against a ₹119 ceiling
- ⚠️ **Learning-limited** — ₹200/day is ₹215 below the recomputed ₹415 floor

> **Recommendation: raise to ₹415/day.** Not because bigger is better, but because ₹415 is `7.143 × 58` — the point at which Meta can accumulate 50 conversations a week and actually optimize.

He raised it. Per the [scaling rules](../playbooks/P12-scaling-ladder.md), a jump from ₹200 to ₹415 is a significant edit that resets learning — so he accepted one more unstable week as the price of getting out of learning permanently.

### Month 2, at ₹415/day

Costs typically fall once an ad set exits learning. Assume ₹48 per conversation:

```
Events per day    = ₹415 ÷ ₹48    = 8.6
Events per week   = 8.6 × 7        = 61        ✅ above 50 → EXITS LEARNING

Monthly ad spend  = ₹415 × 30                  = ₹12,450
GST at 18%                                     = ₹2,241
Total outflow                                  = ₹14,691   ← budget for this
Conversations     = ₹12,450 ÷ ₹48              = 259
Customers at 37.5%                             = 97
Revenue           = 97 × ₹1,800                = ₹1,74,600
Gross profit      = 97 × ₹990                  = ₹96,030
Less ad spend                                  = ₹83,580 contribution
```

> ⚠️ **That last block is a projection, not a promise.** It assumes the ₹48 CPA and 37.5% close rate hold at 2× volume. They often don't — costs usually rise as you scale, because you reach less-ideal prospects. The correct response is to **recompute at each step**, which is exactly what [P12](../playbooks/P12-scaling-ladder.md) instructs.

**Note the GST line.** He planned ₹12,450 and the actual outflow is ₹14,691. Budget for the total, not the ad spend → [`../setup/02-payments-india-gst.md`](../setup/02-payments-india-gst.md).

## Step 8 — What he does next

| Timing | Action |
|---|---|
| Now | Hold ₹415/day. **No edits for 7 days** — learning was just reset |
| Day 14 | Recompute. Check frequency; if above 3, refresh creative |
| Ongoing | Scale **+20% every 4 days** while cost per result stays under ₹119 → [P12](../playbooks/P12-scaling-ladder.md) |
| Parallel | Build `Video 50% - 365d` audience from Ad 2 → cheaper retargeting later ([P07](../playbooks/P07-video-retarget-funnel-200.md)) |
| Stop at | The budget where cost per conversation reaches **₹119** — that's his maximum profitable spend |
| Never | Optimize for website purchases. He has no website, and it'd need ~₹3,000+/day |

---

## The five things this example demonstrates

1. **The budget came from arithmetic, not opinion.** ₹322, then ₹415 — each a multiplication anyone can check. Never "start with ₹500 and see".
2. **The Day-0 estimate was 29% wrong, and the system expected that.** Priors pick a strategy; your own account sets the budget.
3. **Affordability and learnability are different constraints.** He could afford ₹119 leads from day one but still couldn't exit learning at ₹200/day. Both had to be checked.
4. **The honest answer was uncomfortable and useful.** "₹200/day works but stays learning-limited" beats pretending otherwise — and it's why he had a real decision to make on Day 7.
5. **Nothing was edited during learning.** The single most common way small budgets are wasted is reacting on Day 3.
